🌟 What is Tajweed?

Tajweed (تجويد) comes from the Arabic root j-w-d meaning “to make excellent” or “to improve”.

In the science of Quranic recitation, Tajweed means giving every letter its right (ḥaqq) and its due (mustaḥaqq) — pronouncing each letter from its correct articulation point (makhraj) with its proper characteristics (ṣifāt), and applying the rules that arise when letters meet.

Why does it matter?

Allah commands in the Quran:

وَرَتِّلِ ٱلْقُرْءَانَ تَرْتِيلًا

“And recite the Quran with measured recitation (tartīl).” — Surah Al-Muzzammil 73:4

The Prophet ﷺ recited the Quran exactly as it was revealed by Jibrīl (peace be upon him). Tajweed preserves that exact way of recitation so the meaning is not changed by incorrect pronunciation.

Levels of obligation

  • Fard ʿayn (individual obligation): Avoiding clear mistakes (lahn jalī) that change the meaning.
  • Recommended: Mastering finer details of elongation, nasalization, etc.
For stage / beginner students: Start with correct letter sounds and the most common rules (Noon Sākinah, Madd, Qalqalah). Practice short surahs every day. Listening to a skilled reciter and repeating after them is the traditional and most effective method (talaqqī).

Sources: Classical definition of Ibn al-Jazarī; Quran 73:4; standard works such as Tuhfat al-Atfāl and explanations from SeekersGuidance and established Tajweed scholars.

📚 Foundational Tajweed Rules for Beginners

Focus on these first. Master one group before moving to the next.

1. Makhārij al-Ḥurūf — Places of Articulation

Every Arabic letter has a precise exit point. The five main areas are:

ء هـ ع ح غ خ
Throat (Ḥalq)
Deep, middle and upper throat letters. English speakers often need extra practice here.
ق ك ج ش ي ض ل ن ر
Tongue (Lisān)
The largest group. Pay special attention to ض, ظ, ص, ق.
ف ب م و
Lips (Shafatān)
غُنَّة
Nasal passage (Khayshūm)
Home of the nasal sound (ghunnah) on ن and م.
ا و ي
Empty space of the mouth (Jawf)
The three long vowels (madd letters).

2. Rules of Noon Sākinah & Tanwīn

When نْ or a tanwīn (ً ٍ ٌ) is followed by another letter, one of four rules applies:

Iẓhār (Clear) Before the 6 throat letters: ء هـ ع ح غ خ
مِنْ عِلْمٍ
Pronounce the ن clearly, no nasalization.
Idghām (Merging) Before ي ر م ل و ن (Yarmalūn)
مِن رَّبِّهِمْ
Merge with or without ghunnah depending on the letter.
Iqlāb (Conversion) Before ب only
مِنۢ بَعْدِ
Change the ن sound into a light م with ghunnah.
Ikhfāʾ (Hiding) Before the remaining 15 letters
مِن شَرِّ
Hide the ن slightly with a light nasal sound.

3. Other Essential Beginner Rules

Qalqalah Echoing sound on ق ط ب ج د when they have sukūn or at a stop.
أَحَدٌ • ٱلصَّمَدُ
Madd (Elongation) Natural madd (madd ṭabīʿī) = 2 counts on ا و ي.
قَالَ • يَقُولُ • قِيلَ
Ghunnah Nasal sound held for 2 counts on نّ and مّ (shaddah).
إِنَّ • ثُمَّ
Heavy vs Light Letters Heavy (tafkhīm): خ ص ض غ ط ق ظ
The lām in “Allāh” is heavy after fatḥah/ḍammah and light after kasrah.
Practice tip: Read Al-Fātiḥah slowly every day while applying these rules. Record yourself and compare with a clear reciter such as Sheikh Al-Husary or Mishary Alafasy.

🧠 Memorization Helper

Beginners should memorize with correct Tajweed from day one. Use short, frequent sessions.

Recommended order for beginners

  1. Al-Fātiḥah (essential for every prayer)
  2. Al-Ikhlāṣ, Al-Falaq, An-Nās (the three Quls)
  3. Al-Kawthar, Al-ʿAṣr, An-Naṣr, Al-Fīl and other short surahs of Juz ʿAmma

Simple daily method

  • Listen to one ayah 5–7 times
  • Repeat after the reciter 5 times
  • Recite from memory 3 times
  • Connect to the previous ayah
  • Review previous material before adding new
